💻🦖 Final Project Design Document 🥩🦖

Situated learning is among the most effective ways to teach Computer Science. However, for the most effect, individual attention must be lavished on the student: think of the number of professors, TAs working in office hours and course development, the sheer number of hours needed to run a course properly. Currently, these methods cannot be achieved at scale. 🦖

We set out to create a web IDE (integrated development environment) to support introductory Computer Science instruction, by leveraging LLMs’ capacity to respond to dynamic context. Key to introductory CS is learning how to break down a problem, and to attack problems in a structured manner. In such a setup, an LLM would guide progression, diagnose bugs, and provide conceptual clarifications or corrections; the student completes a tangible step of an overall project, and progresses to the next step when they think the current one is complete. Instructions for the next step are generated by the LLM as a function of the code input at the previous step. Depending on this input, instructions may be the next chunked step to complete the project, or targeted feedback to get student solutions in line with ground truth. We believe this is a learning experience grounded in building and iteration, where understanding develops through doing rather than receiving answers. 🦖

AI turns assignment specifications into dynamic instructions based on student progress; this is outsourcing much of the cognitive burden normally placed on an educator, while providing the student with support to complete a project without needing to outsource. The AI embodies the cognitive role of being a teacher or teaching assistant, guiding the student to learn concepts collaboratively and sequentially before arriving at the solution.

While we believe our tool could be applied to many aspects of computer science education, we are designing our experience for an intended learner with little to no programming experience and fairly limited ability to chunk problems in a computer-science context. 🦖
